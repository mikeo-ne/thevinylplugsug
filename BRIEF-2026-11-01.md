```

        │

  Deposit prompt ──▶ wallet approves ──▶ provider says SUCCESSFUL
        │                                     │

        ▼                                     │
  Invoice generated ◀──────────────────────────┘
        │
        ▼

        │
        ▼
  Auto-check: sweep re-polls the wallet, matches amount, flags shortfalls
        │
        ▼
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  GATE: only your confirm click (or MOMO_AUTO_CONFIRM at   │
  │  the full 50%) releases the plant. A successful prompt    │
  │  is never a confirmation.                                 │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
        │
        ▼
  Plant order sheet + email draft, one click, specs pre-filled
```

Two things worth saying plainly, because they shape the build:

**"Auto-check payment" is already real, not a stub.** `POST /api/orders/:id/deposit/request`
sends the collection request and `GET …/deposit/:intentId` polls the provider; a daily
sweep re-polls anything left pending and survives a provider outage. What is missing is
not the check — it is the **paper trail afterwards** (an invoice) and the **hand-off**
(WhatsApp).

**"Draft an order with plant" must stay a draft.** Your note says *draft*, and that
matches the rule you set: nothing reaches a supplier before the deposit is confirmed.
So the build produces the plant order sheet and the addressed email, and stops. The
send button stays behind the gate.

## Point 1 — WhatsApp redirect, USSD prompt, and organised orders & payments

> "Alerts & Orders once made should redirect the order to whatsapp and also be able to
> prompt a payment USSD to make deposit payments and lets find a way of organising these
> orders and payments made."

Three asks in one line. Separately costed:

**1a. Redirect the order to WhatsApp.** After an order is placed and after a deposit is
requested, the client should land in WhatsApp with the order already written out —
not in a blank chat. Today the success screen shows a reference and a phone number and
leaves the client to compose the message themselves.

Build: a `wa.me/256768261003?text=…` deep link on the success screen and the deposit
card, pre-filled with reference, title, quantity, format, deposit due, and the
payment status. On mobile it opens the WhatsApp app; on desktop, WhatsApp Web. No API,
no credentials, no template approval. It is the fastest correct answer and it works
before any vendor paperwork exists.

**1b. Prompt a payment USSD.** Already shipped in the last round: pressing the deposit
button sends a real MTN MoMo / Airtel Money collection request to the payer's handset.
The rails report `ready:false` until `MOMO_SUBSCRIPTION_KEY` / `MOMO_API_USER` /
`MOMO_API_KEY` are in the environment, and when they are unset the button does the
honest thing — it says the rail is not set up and shows the merchant number `0768 261 003`
plus the reference. **What is still missing is the USSD fallback for people on a phone
that never shows the prompt:** a `tel:*165*3%23` style dial link and a copyable
`*165*3#` string, shown only when no prompt could be sent. I would fold that into the
same deposit card rather than a new screen.

**1c. Organise the orders and the payments.** This is the real gap and it is a
navigation problem, not a data problem. Orders live in one console panel, deposit
attempts live inside each order, and there is no single place that answers "what money
came in this week, and what is still owed".

Build: a **Payments** panel in the console, sitting beside Orders and Files.
One table of every payment event — order, client, provider, intent id, amount,
fee, status, confirmed-by, timestamp — filterable by status and date, with a
running total for the month and a CSV export. Plus a `paid` / `owed` figure on each
order row, so the order list itself answers the question.

Done when: you can open `/admin`, filter to "pending", and see every client who owes a
deposit without opening a single order.

Needs: nothing.

## Point 2 — the invoice, and the proof of payment

> "Order → make payment → auto-generate invoice → auto-share / direct to whatsapp to
> show proof of payment → auto-check payment and then draft an order with plant."

The one component that does not exist at all is **the invoice**. There is a quote
(the configurator) and an order record, and the copy in three places says "an invoice
with SWIFT details is issued" — but nothing generates one. That is the hole in the
middle of your diagram.

Build:

- **`GET /api/orders/:id/invoice`** — a server-rendered, print-friendly invoice: your
  letterhead, the order reference, issue date, the full spec (format, colour, sleeve,
  quantity, plant, turnaround), the money broken down all-in, the deposit paid to date
  and the balance, and the payment reference. Print to PDF from the browser, or attach
  it to an email.
- **Numbering and immutability.** Invoices need a sequence (`7H-INV-0001`) and must not
  change once issued. A paid deposit issues a receipt line on the same invoice rather
  than a new document, so the client has one PDF that shows the whole story.
- **The WhatsApp share** becomes one button: `wa.me` with the invoice link, the
  reference and the amount, plus a line inviting them to attach their bank/wallet
  screenshot. That is "show proof of payment" working without building an upload
  inbox — their proof arrives in a chat you already read.
- **Auto-check** already exists (the sweep). After an invoice is issued, the sweep result
  is stamped onto the invoice: "settled 1 Nov, MTN reference …" or "short by UGX 240,000".

Done when: a fresh order produces a numbered invoice within a second of the order
being accepted, the client can open it on their phone, and the balance on it matches
what the wallet actually collected.

Needs: nothing. (A VAT/TIN line if you are registered — see questions.)

## Point 3 — admin-side uploads, artist deals, and honest pre-orders

> "As admin we also need to be able to upload files to press orders coz we actually don't
> have pressed orders in our shop. I want to be able to deal with artists and upload their
> files on our end to place pre-order vinyl in the shop instead of us advertising what we
> don't have."

This is the biggest build in the notes, and the one with a commercial payoff: it turns
`/shop` from a catalogue of five sample sleeves into a storefront that can sell a run
before it exists — which is exactly how pre-order vinyl is normally financed.

Today: uploads are attached to an order created in the same browser session (the chunked
uploader you just got), and `/shop` is a **static list in `shared/shop.js`** with fixed
prices and stock. There is no way for you to upload on someone's behalf, and no way to
add a product.

Build, in three pieces:

**3a. Admin upload.** A "Upload for a client" control in the Files panel and in the order
panel: choose an existing order (or create a draft order for an artist who has not filled
the form), then upload masters and artwork from your own machine with the same chunked
store. Files land in `orders/7H-…/audio` with the same checksum and download link.

**3b. Artist deals and draft orders.** An order needs a state before it is a
client-submitted order. The lifecycle today starts at `received` and runs through
`confirmed → forwarded → in_production → shipped → delivered` (plus `cancelled`), and
there is nothing before `received`. Add `draft` — created by you from a phone call or a
WhatsApp conversation, owned by an artist name and number, priced normally, and
convertible to a live order once the client confirms. This is what lets you "deal with
artists" without asking them to fill a web form.

**3c. Pre-order releases in the shop.** Replace the static catalogue with a store that
is *seeded* from `shared/shop.js` but **extendable from the console**: create a release,
attach the artwork you uploaded, set price, quantity, colour, sleeve, plant and an
expected ship date, and mark it `pre-order`. The storefront then labels it honestly —
"Pre-order · pressing at GZ Media · ships March 2027 · deposit taken today" — instead of
implying a record is on a shelf. A pre-order takes a deposit and records a balance due,
reusing the payment rails you already have; it must **not** go near the retail stock
counts or the fan-delivery flow.

The honesty point matters as much as the feature: the shop currently opens with five
records that are samples. Once you can create releases, I would also add a one-line
`isSample` flag so the demo catalogue can be hidden on production without deleting it.

Done when: you can create a release, upload the artist's master and artwork from the
console, and see it live on `/shop` as a pre-order taking deposits — with the card
saying what is real and what is still being pressed.

Needs: ~2 rounds. The payment plan for the 50%-then-balance split needs a decision
(see questions).

## Point 4 — WhatsApp alerts

> "If I can receive order alerts on email and telegram but I really need to add whatsapp
> token and ID because I can't seem to be able to create these."

The app already has a WhatsApp Cloud API channel written (`deliverWhatsApp`,
`WHATSAPP_TOKEN`, `WHATSAPP_PHONE_ID`, template `new_vinyl_order`). It has never fired
because those two variables do not exist. Here is why you could not create them, and
what to do.

**Why it feels impossible:** WhatsApp alerts are not like Telegram. There is no bot to
message and copy a token from. You need a Meta Business portfolio, a Meta app, the
WhatsApp product added to it, a phone number that can receive an SMS, and — for messages
you start, as opposed to messages a client starts — an **approved template**. Also: on a
brand-new business account, Meta gives you a **test number** that can only message five
verified recipients until the business is verified.

**The path, in order:**

1. `business.facebook.com` → create a Business portfolio for 7H Music Group (legal name
   and address, not a nickname). Business verification takes a few days and is required
   before you can message arbitrary numbers.
2. `developers.facebook.com` → create app, type **Business**. Add the **WhatsApp**
   product.
3. The WhatsApp → API Setup page shows a **temporary token** (~24h) and a
   **Phone number ID**. That pair is enough to prove the channel — paste them in and send
   a test alert to your own number, which must be added to the five-recipient list first.
4. For a permanent token: Business settings → **System users** → add a system user →
   assign the app → generate a token with `whatsapp_business_messaging` and
   `whatsapp_business_management`. That token is what goes in `WHATSAPP_TOKEN`.
5. Register your own number (256 768 261003) as the sender if you want alerts to appear
   from the business rather than from a test number.
6. Submit the `new_vinyl_order` template (order reference, title, amount) for approval —
   usually minutes to a day.

**Meanwhile, the build does two things.** Click-to-chat links (point 1a) carry the
client-side traffic with no token at all, and the console stops treating a missing
WhatsApp token as a fault: it will say "WhatsApp: click-to-chat mode — token not
configured" as **information**, not a red row in the alert log. The `unconfigured`
classification already exists for exactly this; the change is to stop it reading like a
failure and to stop the daily summary repeating it.

Done when: with the token set, a test alert arrives on your phone; without it, nothing
in the console looks broken.

Needs: the Meta steps above, which only you can do (they are tied to your business
identity).

## Point 5 — whether clients should see the 25%

> "Is it a good idea that clients can tell and see that we get a 25% package charge,
> what if they decided to reach out to the plants themselves rather than me charging them
> for something they already can do on their own."

Short answer: **no, not as a percentage line.** You have already decided the fix; here is
the reasoning so the decision survives contact with a client who asks.

The line `Management fee · 25%` appears in the quote summary and the success page, next
to `freight` and `plant cost`. Presented that way it reads as a markup on a service the
client believes they can buy themselves — which is exactly the thought you had. Two
things are true at once: 25% on the factory cost is a normal broker margin, and showing
it as a separate line invites the comparison.

The reason the comparison is not actually fair is that the client is not buying the
pressing. They are buying: mastering pre-flight for the format, artwork converted to the
plant's CMYK and bleed, plant choice and negotiation, scheduling and proof chasing,
freight and customs into Kampala, and someone to call when 500 sleeves arrive with the
wrong spine. Your FAQ already says this well.

So the change is presentation, not arithmetic:

- **Client-facing documents** (quote, invoice, receipt, WhatsApp summary) show
  `Pressing, freight & handling — all-in` and one total. The fee line disappears; the
  total does not change.
- **The console** keeps the margin visible per order, in the Payments panel and on the
  order sheet, so you are never guessing what you made.
- **The FAQ** keeps explaining what is included, in plain language, without naming a
  percentage. That is where a curious client gets the honest answer.
- The word "brokerage" stays in the tagline. Brokering is the service; a percentage is
  just a way of pricing it.

Done when: a copy-paste of the quote page contains no percentage fee, and `/admin` still
shows it.

Needs: nothing. ~half a round, mostly touching `OrderSummary`, `SuccessPage`,
`Configurator` and the invoice.

## Point 6 — six plants, one spec sheet

> "Also noticed I have to manually go to all the plant websites to make an order and also
> fill in order specs but I don't think I can keep doing that over and over and remember,
> we have 6 plants."

Blunt version: **none of the six plants offers a public ordering API.** Mobineko, Green
Lakes, Seabass, Dr. Dub, GZ Media and Deepgrooves all quote and order through web forms,
email or a sales rep. Anything that claims to "submit to the plant" would be scraping a
form and would break the first time a site is redesigned.

So the win is not automation of the submission. It is **never typing the same spec
twice**, and **never opening six websites to find out which one to use**:

**6a. One generated order sheet per plant.** Every field a plant asks for — title,
artist, quantity, format, weight, colour, sleeve type, print, labels, matrix/runout,
masters (with the download link and checksum), artwork, pack-out, shipping address,
deadline — rendered from the order record you already have, with the plant's own email
address already in the "to" field. Printable to PDF, or sent as the draft email the app
already builds behind the deposit gate. You fill the gaps once per plant; the shared
fields never get retyped.

**6b. Per-plant profiles.** Each plant has quirks and I would store them: minimums
(Dr. Dub is lathe-only below 25, GZ starts at 250), which formats they take, lead times,
whether they want a specific file naming convention, and your rep's email. Then the
order sheet adapts: a 50-unit seven-inch auto-excludes GZ and Deepgrooves, and only
shows the plants that can actually do the job.

**6c. One comparison table.** For any quote, all six plants side by side — landed cost,
turnaround, and what they are good at — so the choice happens once, on one screen,
instead of by opening six tabs.

**6d. A specs sheet you fill once.** An artist/record profile (artist name, title, ISRCs,
label name, copyright lines, genre) that every plant sheet, invoice and sleeve print-out
inherits. Fill it at order creation, never again.

Done when: a confirmed order produces, in one click, a complete sheet for each eligible
plant with the master link embedded and the rep addressed — and you have not opened a
plant website.

Needs: ~1 round. Optional later: reading each plant's price list into the comparison, but
their prices are quote-based, so this stays manual and honest.
